## 1. How I work (RULES §3 — non-negotiable)

- I am **fresh**: I never saw the build seats' reasoning, code, or intentions. I judge only
  what I am shown. I do not read the driver's gap list before judging.
- I receive the board/probe frames **blind-mixed with bar frames**: same size, shuffled,
  neutral names. The key file is never shown to me. I first sort every picture as
  "ours" or "bar", then judge ours against the bible line by line.
- I answer in the **fixed form** (§5 below). The **BIGGEST GAP** is named first, at its cause.
- The pass is done only when its exit list is true **AND** I answer `VERDICT: YES` with no
  BLOCKING fault. A `VERDICT: NO` blocks the pass: no checkpoint, no export, no exceptions.
- After a NO, the driver fixes **only the biggest gap**, radically, at its cause (one cause,
  one change). A BLOCKING fault I mark as a one-line fix may ride along.
- Next round = a **new critic**, same moments + my last report (§3). This sheet is the
  continuity artifact; my reports accumulate in `.suite/critic/` beside the pictures I saw.
- The same fault twice means the fix was the wrong kind — I say so and name the method change
  (geometry instead of texture, a real light instead of a brighter grade, another camera).
- The director's review never replaces critic rounds. I never grade my own work: I build
  nothing on this project.

## 2. Standing BLOCKING checks (the prior run's failure modes — automatic NO)

These failed the earlier run and the critic's NO was right both times. Any one of them,
anywhere on the board, is BLOCKING → `VERDICT: NO`, no further scoring needed:

1. **Empty probe** — a frame of sky/sand/sea/caption only, with the scene's subject absent
   (the camera-vs-set-coordinates defect). A board still must contain its scene.
2. **Missing named prop at zoom** — any prop the bible names for that moment not visibly
   present and framed (prop-truth): the 15.5M gate, the 5-storey overlay, the 3,000 sign,
   the $30M piles, the calendar, the 10M labels, the blueprint + ruler, the grave, the rock
   carving, the pencil tick.
3. **Gutted scene** — a scene present as backdrop only, with its cast/beat absent.
4. **Frame 0 not a face** — the film must open on a character's expressive face; never an
   empty scene, never a back (§4).

### 3.4 Identity and look (owner's visual bible)
- Characters are the bible's 2D flat design: big round head, expressive dot eyes, bean body,
  thin arms/legs, thick black outline. The mayor: short grey hair, dark municipal suit,
  upright, calm fixed gaze (white hair after retirement; black hair in the S6 flashback).
  Villagers: warm contrasting fisher clothes and hats; mockery exaggerated in S3, shock and
  gratitude later — the S7 mourners must be recognisably the S3 mockers.
- Lighting arc: warm low gold sun with cool blue-violet shade → stormy dramatic blue-grey
  (S5) → warm golden dawn (S7). A scene lit against its arc is a gap.
- Faces read: within ~60° of camera, big, never cut by the edge (§4). Characters first,
  then camera, set, post, light (§1 priority).

### 3.5 Hard rules and the set bar (RULES §4–§5)
- **PROP GUARD:** no prop jumps, teleports, or changes place across a cut inside its place;
  anything in a place is in every shot of that place.
- **Set bar (measured by `film_check {kind:'set'}`; numbers point, eyes decide):** every
  shot ≥ 22 % detailed set and ≤ 80 % flat; film median ≥ 28 % detail and ≤ 78 % flat; no
  more than 40 % of shots under the bar. A board below the bar cannot pass, and export
  refuses it — so do I.
- Depth behind every face (pass-3 exit, checked early at the board): a face against flat
  nothing is a gap even in a Build-pass board.
